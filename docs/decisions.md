# Decisions

Append-only log of project decisions. Each entry: date, decision, alternatives considered, reason.

## 2026-09-19: Event data sources
Decision: Use StatsBomb open data for 2015/16 (La Liga, Premier League, Serie A, Ligue 1) and the public Wyscout dataset for 2017/18 (Serie A, Premier League, La Liga, Ligue 1, Bundesliga) as the two event-data seasons. National-team competitions are excluded.
Alternatives considered: one provider only; aggregated statistics from Understat or FBref (deferred).
Reason: StatsBomb's open data has five league-wide competition-seasons (the four above and the Indian Super League 2021/22) and 49 single-team ones, with no consecutive full league seasons. Wyscout adds a second full season and the Bundesliga.

## 2026-09-19: Transfermarkt for transfers and costs
Decision: Use the Transfermarkt dataset compiled by dcaribou (CC0 repository licence) for transfers, appearances and valuations. Raw data is downloaded locally and never committed. Player costs will come from market valuations, not transfer fees.
Alternatives considered: transfer fees as costs.
Reason: The transfers table has a positive fee for only 17,554 of 175,165 rows and no loan flag.

## 2026-09-19: Minutes come from match lineups
Decision: Player minutes are computed from each provider's match lineups, not taken from Transfermarkt.
Alternatives considered: Transfermarkt appearance minutes.
Reason: Minutes must describe the same matches the event features are built from, and the minimum-minutes filter must be enforceable on them.

## 2026-09-19: Wyscout limits
Decision: Wyscout-based features exclude pressing intensity, and results from the two providers are compared as relationships, not levels.
Alternatives considered: a pressing proxy built from other event types.
Reason: Wyscout's public data has no pressure events, and its event density is about half of StatsBomb's (about 1,670 events per game in ten measured games, against about 3,400 in the 2015/16 La Liga).

## 2026-09-19: Environment
Decision: Python 3.12, with multimethod pinned to 1.10 and the other dependency versions pinned in pyproject.toml.
Alternatives considered: Python 3.13 (socceraction 1.5.x requires Python below 3.13); multimethod 2.x (it breaks pandera 0.17.2, which socceraction's schemas use).
Reason: Both constraints were hit while loading StatsBomb data.

## 2026-09-19: Linking StatsBomb players to Transfermarkt
Decision: Map each StatsBomb team to one Transfermarkt club per league by an optimal one-to-one assignment on club-name similarity, then match players by normalised name or nickname within the mapped club and season (similarity 0.85, margin 0.05). A second pass accepts a Transfermarkt name whose tokens are all contained in the StatsBomb name and nickname tokens.
Alternatives considered: mapping each team to its best name match independently; the published record-linkage tables.
Reason: The independent mapping sent 5 of 80 teams to the wrong club and left two leagues below a 90% minutes-weighted match rate (overall 90.63%). The one-to-one mapping gives 96.78% (La Liga 94.82%, Premier League 97.45%, Serie A 97.97%, Ligue 1 96.88%), and every mapped team shares at least 19 players by exact name with its club. The published link tables could not be retrieved when tried. The second pass adds 62 pairs and 2.32 minutes-weighted points, and Transfermarkt minutes are within 0.8 to 1.2 times StatsBomb minutes for 93.98% of matched pairs.

## 2026-09-19: Linking Wyscout players to Transfermarkt
Decision: Link Wyscout players to Transfermarkt players by exact date of birth plus name similarity (first and last name, or short name; similarity 0.75; exactly one candidate at or above it). A second pass accepts a same-birthday candidate whose name tokens are all contained in the Wyscout name tokens.
Alternatives considered: matching on names alone; the published record-linkage tables.
Reason: Exact date of birth is a strong key. The first pass links 93.41% of the Wyscout players with 450 or more minutes (Spain 84.16%, the other four leagues 95.05% to 97.15%; the misses are mostly long legal names). For linked players, Transfermarkt minutes are within 0.8 to 1.2 times Wyscout minutes in 99.9% of cases, which supports the links without a ground truth. The second pass raises the rate to 97.06% (Spain 95.27%).

## 2026-09-19: Transfers, loans and the transfer cohorts
Decision: The Transfermarkt transfers table has no loan flag, so each mover in the summer window is labelled by whether the same player has a mirror move (clubs swapped) within three years: paid, paid_mirror, loan_return, loan_out, both or free_other. The primary transfer cohort is permanent moves: paid, paid_mirror, and free_other not dated 30 June. Loan endings and moves labelled both are excluded; loan-outs are kept as a sensitivity set.
Alternatives considered: treating every mover in the window as a transfer; flagging loans by whether the player reappears for the old club two seasons later.
Reason: In the summer 2016 cohort 64 of 186 movers, and in summer 2018 118 of 321, are dated 30 June with a fee of zero or null, and the mirror label identifies 62 of the 64 and all 118 as loan endings. The reappearance heuristic flags only 7 of 32 and 9 of 66 loan-outs. At the last funnel stage (linked, 450 or more minutes the next season, destination in the same leagues) the permanent cohorts number 54 and 108.

## 2026-09-19: Correction to the StatsBomb minutes-agreement figure
Decision: Judge the StatsBomb links by minutes agreement among matched pairs with 450 or more StatsBomb minutes, as for Wyscout.
Alternatives considered: all matched pairs.
Reason: The earlier entry reports 93.98% of all matched pairs within 0.8 to 1.2 times. The farthest pairs there are players with fewer than 60 minutes, where a few minutes of difference is a large ratio. Among pairs with 450 or more StatsBomb minutes the share is 99.94% (median ratio 0.954); the Wyscout figure on the same basis is 99.9%.

## 2026-09-19: Earliest whole transfer row per mover
Decision: Each mover in the window is represented by the player's earliest qualifying transfer as a whole row.
Alternatives considered: taking the first non-null value of each column, which the first version did.
Reason: The old rule could mix two transfers: when the earliest transfer had a null fee, it took the fee of a later one. No mover in either cohort triggers this. All 10 players with more than one qualifying transfer (1 in the summer 2016 cohort, 9 in summer 2018) have a fee of 0.0 on the earliest, and the legacy and corrected rules give identical rows, labels and funnel levels.

## 2026-09-19: Within-season matchday split
Decision: Each StatsBomb 2015/16 league is split by matchday. Window 1 is matchdays 1 to ceil(0.6 × the league's last matchday), window 2 the rest. All four leagues run to matchday 38, so the boundary is matchday 23 everywhere: 230 and 150 games per window (Ligue 1 228 and 149), about 60.1% to 60.4% of actions in window 1. No game has a missing matchday. Leave-one-league-out across the four leagues is the second split.
Alternatives considered: splitting by calendar date.
Reason: A forward holdout inside one season gives features and a later target for the same players without needing a second season. Matchday order and date order differ only in La Liga: one window-1 game (matchday 16, played 17 February 2016) falls after the first window-2 game, and 10 window-2 games are dated before it; the largest gap is 4.88 days. The Premier League, Serie A and Ligue 1 have no violations. The split is left unchanged.

## 2026-09-19: Leakage-safe action values
Decision: The VAEP scores and concedes classifiers are fit five times, each on window-1 actions only. The pooled fit uses all four leagues and rates every action in all four. Each leave-one-league-out fit uses the other three leagues and rates only the actions of the league it left out. Features are the installed socceraction defaults (568 columns, three previous actions). The learner is xgboost 2.1.4 with tree_method hist, random_state 0, n_jobs 16, objective binary:logistic and 100 rounds, otherwise library defaults, with no tuning. Refitting the pooled scores model gives a bit-identical window-2 Brier score (0.009096510708332062). The installed default feature set includes the current action's result, so VAEP here is a retrospective value of what the action did, not an expected value of the attempt.
Alternatives considered: one fit on all actions; socceraction's own tree settings (depth 3 with early stopping on a random 25% of training rows).
Reason: A window-2 target must come from a model that never trained on window-2 actions, and a leave-one-league-out target from one that never saw that league. Evaluated on rows each model did not train on, mean predicted scores probability is within 6.1% of the observed rate everywhere (pooled on window 2: 0.010410 against 0.010746, Brier 0.009097, log loss 0.047711). Concedes probability is under-predicted: pooled on window 2 0.002103 against 0.002274 (−7.5%, Brier 0.002190). Three leave-one-league-out evaluations miss by more than 10%: La Liga window 1 (−14.9%), La Liga window 2 (−13.2%) and Ligue 1 window 2 (−14.3%). Every reliability table under-predicts in the lowest deciles and over-predicts in the top decile (pooled scores: 0.0584 predicted against 0.0539 observed).

## 2026-09-19: Minutes filter for window pairs
Decision: A player-team enters a window-1 to window-2 pair with at least 450 minutes in window 1 and 270 in window 2. That gives 1,144 pairs in the pooled table: La Liga 299, Premier League 278, Serie A 280, Ligue 1 287.
Alternatives considered: window-1 minimums of 180, 270, 360 and 450 crossed with window-2 minimums of 180, 270 and 360. Pair counts run from 1,335 (180 and 180) to 1,073 (450 and 360).
Reason: 450 minutes is the project's existing minimum for a player season. In window 2, 270 minutes is three full matches and 20% of the 1,350-minute maximum; raising the window-2 minimum from 180 to 270 costs 46 pairs. The threshold was chosen on minutes and cohort size, not on the correlations below. As a pipeline diagnostic only (no shrinkage, no controls, no held-out structure), the correlation between window-1 and window-2 pooled VAEP/90 ranges from 0.3244 to 0.3851 (Pearson) and 0.2671 to 0.3047 (Spearman) across the grid, and is 0.3807 and 0.3018 at the chosen threshold. It is not a result.

## 2026-09-20: Recalibration method
Decision: Scores and concedes probabilities are recalibrated with a logistic map on the logit, p_cal = 1 / (1 + exp(-(a + b * logit(p)))), fit by unpenalized maximum likelihood, one map per rating model, league and label.
Alternatives considered: isotonic regression.
Reason: Out of sample the raw classifiers are over-dispersed (for the pooled scores model on window 2, the lowest decile is under-predicted by a factor of 2.65 and the top decile over-predicted), and action values are differences of these probabilities. Isotonic regression was rejected because it is piecewise constant: consecutive states in the same block would get identical probabilities and a value change of exactly zero, and one league's window 1 holds only 932 to 1,085 concedes positives to fit on.

## 2026-09-20: Calibration data
Decision: Every calibrator is fit on window-1 rows only, using predictions from a classifier that never trained on those rows: for the pooled model, out-of-fold predictions from five game-level folds, the pooled model itself unchanged; for each leave-one-league-out model, its own predictions for its held-out league. Window-2 outcomes enter no fitted component and are used only to report calibration.
Alternatives considered: fitting calibrators on window 2; fitting the pooled calibrators on the pooled model's own window-1 predictions.
Reason: Fitting on window 2 would use the target, and the pooled model's own window-1 predictions are in-sample. Folds are whole games because labels and features span neighbouring actions within a game and each value is a difference of consecutive states.

## 2026-09-20: Leakage rule for leave-one-league-out, amended
Decision: The classifier never trains on the held-out league; its calibrator may be fit on that league's window-1 outcomes; neither uses window 2. The leave-one-league-out result therefore measures transfer to a new league after recalibration on that league's first 23 matchdays, and is described that way. The earlier leakage-rule entry still governs the classifier.
Alternatives considered: a calibrator fit on the three training leagues.
Reason: Miscalibration is league-specific (concedes under-predicted by 13% to 15% for held-out La Liga and Ligue 1), so a calibrator fit on the training leagues would not correct the held-out one.

## 2026-09-20: Pooled window-1 values
Decision: Pooled window-1 action values come from calibrated out-of-fold predictions and window-2 values from the calibrated pooled model; the uncalibrated tables are kept and the calibrated tables are written beside them.
Alternatives considered: calibrating the pooled model's own window-1 predictions; replacing the uncalibrated tables.
Reason: The pooled model trained on every window-1 row, so its window-1 predictions are in-sample (window-1 log loss in-sample against out-of-fold: scores 0.039959 against 0.046413, concedes 0.009418 against 0.013928), and a calibrator learned on out-of-sample predictions does not describe them.

## 2026-09-20: Calibration fit at depth 6
Decision: Compare classifier depth 6 with depth 3, under a rule fixed in advance, before building on calibrated values.
Alternatives considered: keeping the depth-6 two-parameter calibration as it is; a more flexible calibration map.
Reason: On its own fit rows, the two-parameter map leaves the depth-6 concedes probabilities with an S-shaped residual (window 1, all four leagues: the lowest four deciles under-predicted by 16% to 65%, the sixth to ninth deciles over-predicted by 10% to 31%), and the lowest two scores deciles under-predicted by 38% and 25%. Well-calibrated league maps would give a well-calibrated mixture, so the residual comes from the map's shape. Near zero the map behaves like p raised to a power below one, which steepens differences between small probabilities, and action values are built from those differences.

## 2026-09-20: Classifier depth
Decision: Scores use depth 3 and concedes use depth 3, chosen by a rule fixed before the comparison: for each label, depth 3 replaces depth 6 if its calibrated window-1 out-of-fold log loss is no higher and its Hosmer-Lemeshow statistic is lower (ten equal-count bins, all four leagues together); depth 6 stays if it is no worse on both. Only the tree depth changes. The depth is chosen once, by window-1 cross-validation across all four leagues, and used for every model of that label, including leave-one-league-out; no model or choice uses window 2. The depth-6 models, predictions and tables are kept. This amends the entry "Leakage-safe action values".
Alternatives considered: depth 6 for scores; depth 6 for concedes.
Reason: Calibrated window-1 out-of-fold log loss, depth 6 against depth 3: scores 0.046221 against 0.045498, concedes 0.013728 against 0.013315; Hosmer-Lemeshow: scores 104.43 against 14.20, concedes 303.92 against 48.45. Depth 6 overfits (window-1 log loss in-sample against out-of-fold: 0.039959 against 0.046413 for scores, 0.009418 against 0.013928 for concedes), and depth 3 is socceraction's default.

## 2026-09-20: Recalibration kept at depth 3
Decision: The depth-3 logistic recalibration of the scores and concedes probabilities is kept, and every value in the shrinkage and evaluation work is built from the recalibrated v2 player-window tables. The eight leave-one-league-out depth-3 maps were refit on their own rows with their own settings before anything was built on them: all eight came back bit-identical to the stored ones, largest absolute residual 7.86e-14.
Alternatives considered: dropping calibration and rating from the raw depth-3 probabilities; refitting calibration on a different window.
Reason: The depth-3 maps are a league-level correction, chiefly to La Liga concedes, whose raw depth-3 window-1 predictions run 7.4% low pooled and 10.4% low leave-one-league-out, and the remaining bottom-decile concedes floor stands at 107 observed against 64.6 expected. The cost is that La Liga's concedes map steepens small differences by about 20% pooled and 26% leave-one-league-out at p = 0.0007. Reported but not used to choose: on window 2 calibration raised concedes log loss in all nine evaluations, by at most 0.00002 per action. Those four figures are read from the phase 4b2 record and were not recomputed here; the refit check above was.

## 2026-09-20: Shrinkage model and sampling variance
Decision: Window-1 VAEP per 90 is shrunk by a Fay-Herriot model fit separately in each position group. The design is four league indicator columns plus log(window-1 minutes / 1000) and no separate intercept (p = 5); the variance component comes from the Prasad-Rao moment estimator, tau2 = max(0, (sum of squared OLS residuals - sum of (1 - h_jj) psi_j) / (m - p)); the coefficients then come from weighted least squares with weights 1 / (tau2 + psi); the posterior mean is x b + tau2 / (tau2 + psi) * (y - x b). A second variant drops the minutes slope (p = 4). The sampling variance is psi_i = 8100 * phi_g / m_i, with phi_g estimated from game-level dispersion inside the group: per player r_i = sum of game values over sum of game minutes, S_i = sum over games of (v_ik - r_i * m_ik)^2 / m_ik, and phi_g = sum S_i / sum (n_i - 1). Position groups are GK, CB, FB, MID, WIDE and FWD, taken from the group holding the most window-1 lineup minutes and, where a player-team has no positioned window-1 minutes, from the linked Transfermarkt sub-position.
Alternatives considered: one fit pooled across positions; a sampling variance taken from the cross-sectional spread of per-90 rates rather than from within-player game dispersion.
Reason: Dispersion differs by a factor of twenty across groups. On the pooled branch phi is 0.000177 for GK, 0.000563 for CB, 0.000570 for FB, 0.000803 for MID, 0.002111 for WIDE and 0.003524 for FWD, so one common sampling variance would over-shrink defenders and under-shrink forwards; the mean shrinkage factor at the fitted tau^2 runs from 0.058 for FWD to 0.365 for WIDE and GK. Reliability at 450 window-1 minutes is 0.115 for CB, 0.170 for FB, 0.149 for MID, 0.195 for WIDE, 0.144 for GK and 0.023 for FWD, and roughly doubles at 900 minutes. A split-half check on odd against even matchdays, reported without a threshold, gives observed over implied variance of 0.85 for CB, 0.77 for FB, 0.76 for FWD, 0.83 for GK, 1.07 for MID and 0.92 for WIDE, so the game-level estimate is the right order and, outside MID, slightly conservative.

## 2026-09-20: Evaluation design
Decision: The estimation population is the window-1 rows of the player-window table with at least 450 window-1 minutes, 1,401 player-team rows; the threshold is on window minutes, never on season minutes, and nothing fitted reads a window-2 value. Targets and predictors are centred by league and window with c(league, window) = 90 * sum of vaep over sum of minutes across every row of that league and window: pooled, c is 0.135286 and 0.141913 for La Liga, 0.139390 and 0.144441 for the Premier League, 0.133111 and 0.129016 for Serie A, and 0.126290 and 0.137207 for Ligue 1, windows 1 and 2. The score is a Brown-style total squared error, TSE(P) = sum over pairs of (t_i - P_i)^2 - psi2_i, with psi2_i = 8100 * phi_g / m2_i and phi_g estimated on window 1. The pre-registered claim, fixed before any evaluation number was computed, is that shrinkage helps if the upper end of the 95% bootstrap interval for TSE(P3) / TSE(P2) is below 1 on the pooled branch, outfield.
Alternatives considered: a minutes threshold on season minutes; plain squared error with no variance correction; choosing the headline comparison after seeing the results.
Reason: A season-minutes threshold would select on window-2 playing time and so on the target. The correction removes the part of squared error no predictor can remove: on pooled outfield pairs the P3 against P2 ratio is 0.469 corrected and 0.809 uncorrected, so the uncorrected number understates the gap between the baselines by most of its size. Centring by league and window keeps the comparison about ordering inside a league rather than about the level that league and window sit at, which the leave-one-league-out branch shifts. The evaluation set is the 1,144 pairs at 450 window-1 and 270 window-2 minutes: 1,067 outfield, 76 goalkeepers reported separately, and 1 row whose position group is unknown, excluded from both.

## 2026-09-20: Baselines
Decision: Five baselines, all centred by subtracting c(league, 1). P0 is zero after centring, the league level itself. P1 is the Fay-Herriot regression mean x b. P2 is window-1 VAEP per 90, the persistence baseline and the denominator of every ratio. P3 is the posterior mean of the p = 5 fit, the primary predictor. P4 is the posterior mean of the no-minutes-slope fit. No goals-based or expected-goals-based predictor is included.
Alternatives considered: adding goals per 90 or an expected-goals rate as a sixth baseline.
Reason: Every baseline has to predict the same quantity as the target, centred VAEP per 90, and P0 to P4 are all transformations of the same window-1 rating, so the differences between them isolate what the shrinkage does rather than which rating is used. On pooled outfield pairs the TSE ratios to P2 are 0.793 for P0, 0.675 for P1, 0.469 for P3 and 0.441 for P4, which separates the league level, the regression mean and the shrunk estimate cleanly. An expected-goals predictor is not available inside this build: the action tables carry only game, event, period, time, team, player, start and end coordinates, type, result and body part, with no shot-quality field, so it would have to come from a model this build does not fit or from a figure produced outside it, and both are ruled out.

## 2026-09-20: Bootstrap unit
Decision: Uncertainty comes from a cluster bootstrap whose unit is a team within its league, twenty clusters per league and eighty in all. Each replicate draws each league's twenty teams with replacement and a team drawn twice contributes its rows twice; 2,000 replicates from a fixed seed. Every replicate recomputes the league levels, the per-group dispersion, both Fay-Herriot fits, all five predictors and every metric from the drawn rows alone. The action-value classifiers and the calibrators are held fixed, so the intervals are conditional on them. The headline is the pooled-over-leagues outfield interval; per-league intervals are reported flagged as resting on that league's twenty teams alone.
Alternatives considered: resampling players; resampling games; refitting the classifiers and calibrators inside each replicate.
Reason: Teammates share opponents, a schedule and a way of playing, so player rows inside a team are not independent and a player bootstrap would give intervals that are too narrow. Games are not a usable unit because both the 450-minute threshold and the window-1 rate are defined over the whole window. Refitting the rating models per replicate would change what is being measured: the question here is how much shrinkage adds on top of a fixed rating. On the pooled branch, outfield, TSE(P3) / TSE(P2) has a 95% interval of [0.197, 0.748], so the claim passes; leave-one-league-out gives [0.221, 0.730]. Per-league intervals are far wider and the ratio is unstable wherever TSE(P2) comes near zero inside a replicate: Ligue 1 gives [0.112, 1.387] on the pooled branch, and on the goalkeeper subset, where the corrected TSE is near or below zero, the ratio cannot be read as a ratio at all.

## 2026-09-21: Corrections to five entries of 2026-09-20
Decision: Five entries recorded on 2026-09-20 are corrected here rather than edited, since this log is append-only. "Recalibration kept at depth 3": the alternative weighed was dropping recalibration at depth 3, under which leave-one-league-out would have become transfer with no held-out-league outcomes used and La Liga concedes would have stayed 7% to 10% low; recalibrating on another window was not considered. "Shrinkage model and sampling variance": the alternatives weighed were a normal prior with hyperparameters fit by maximum likelihood, rejected because in Brown's (2008) first-half to second-half batting test likelihood-based empirical Bayes did worse than moment and nonparametric estimators; nonparametric empirical Bayes, deferred and recorded as a limitation, since it would pull low-minute players with extreme rates in less; a separate sampling variance for each player, rejected because five to twenty-three games per player make it noisy; and summed squared action values, rejected because actions within a game are not independent. The log-minutes slope was included because sampling variance is set by minutes and coaches choose the minutes; out of sample it did not help (TSE ratio to persistence 0.469 with it, 0.441 without, with no interval on the difference). The "shrinkage factor" in that entry is the weight on the player's own rate, tau2 / (tau2 + psi). "Evaluation design": the variance correction assumes window-1 dispersion describes window-2 noise, and the split-half check, below 1 in five of six groups, suggests it overstates that noise, which flatters the corrected ratio; the uncorrected ratio is the conservative measure and also clears 1 (P3 against P2 0.809, 95% interval 0.722 to 0.910 pooled, 0.726 to 0.908 leave-one-league-out). Centring removes league-and-window level shifts that no player-level predictor can anticipate; the window-2 level is computed from window-2 data as part of the target and enters no fitted component. Pairs join on league, player and team, so the 21 players whose qualifying rows sit at different teams in the two windows are outside the evaluation set. "Baselines": goals-based and expected-goals-based predictors are deferred to the ablation as their own tier, not ruled out; they are not baselines here because a predictor of a different quantity, scored against a VAEP target, is handicapped by construction, and the missing shot-quality field in the cached action tables is a fact about the cache, not the reason. "Bootstrap unit": games were rejected because resampling games keeps every team in every replicate, so team-level variation never enters the intervals; refitting the rating models in each replicate was rejected on cost, about 100 seconds per fit in phase 4b2.
Alternatives considered: editing the five entries in place.
Reason: The log is append-only, and the alternatives and reasons first recorded for these entries were not the ones weighed when the decisions were made.

## 2026-09-21: StatsBomb pressure events
Decision: The Pressure events of the 1,517 league games of 2015/16 were read once from StatsBomb open data, through the same loader as the original ingest, and kept in data/interim/statsbomb/<league>/pressures.parquet: game, event, period, time, team, player, position, location, duration, counterpress flag, possession number, and the pressed player, taken as the player of the first related event from the other team. Every game's re-read event count matched the count stored at ingest; 491,104 pressure events in all.
Alternatives considered: keeping the full event stream; leaving pressing out of the style profile.
Reason: SPADL has no pressure action, so the pressing dimension of the style profile, and later the joint-pressure edges of the player graph, need the raw events. Resolving the pressed player while each whole game is in memory means the full stream need not be read again. Pressing is StatsBomb-only: the Wyscout public data has no pressure events.

## 2026-09-21: Style profile dimensions
Decision: Eight dimensions per window-1 player-team row, from SPADL actions played left to right on a 105 by 68 pitch, except pressing. Open play means passes, crosses, take-ons, carries (SPADL dribbles), shots and bad touches; set pieces are left out of every dimension, because taking them is a duty rather than a style. A sequence is a maximal run of consecutive actions by one team in one period. (1) Pressing: pressure events in the opponent half per 90 minutes, times 0.5 over the opponent's share of in-possession actions in the games played, from StatsBomb events. (2) Possession: the share of open-play actions inside sequences longer than 10 seconds. (3) Build-up speed: minus the mean seconds from the start of a sequence that begins outside the final third to its first action ending inside it, over sequences the player took part in. (4) Width: the mean distance of open-play actions from the pitch's long axis. (5) Verticality: the share of passed distance on forward passes and crosses. (6) Defensive depth: the mean position along the pitch of tackles, interceptions and clearances, over pitch length. (7) Dribbling: take-ons as a share of open-play actions. (8) Shot creation: the share of open-play actions after which the next actions in the same sequence are all by one teammate, ending in his shot.
Alternatives considered: the original project's definitions as written; per-90 counts for every dimension.
Reason: Seven dimensions use SPADL alone and so carry over to the Wyscout data; pressing does not. Compared with the original, dribbling counts take-ons rather than carries and drops a success-rate scaling that measured quality rather than style; width drops an importance weighting the original never defined; build-up speed uses minus the mean rather than the inverse, which is undefined at a mean of zero; pressing is adjusted for the opponent's possession; and the other dimensions are shares, so a team's possession does not pass for a player's style. Quality measures are left out so the ablation can separate how a player plays from how well. Shot creation is flagged: shot-creating actions carry high action values, so it is the dimension closest to the VAEP target.

## 2026-09-21: Team profiles from team actions
Decision: A team's style profile is computed from the team's own window-1 actions, sequences and pressure events with the same definitions, pressing per game rather than per 90 player minutes, instead of as the minutes-weighted average of its players' profiles.
Alternatives considered: the minutes-weighted average of player profiles, as in the original project.
Reason: Averaging across eleven positions pulls every club toward the league centre and removes the between-club variation a fit measure needs. In the original project the tactical weight moved squad impact by under 0.1% for weights from 0 to 0.8, consistent with averaged profiles that carried little signal.

## 2026-09-21: Standardizing style profiles
Decision: Player dimensions are standardized within position group, with the mean and standard deviation of the group's estimation-population rows (window-1 minutes of at least 450), applied to every row of the group; rows of unknown group get no standard scores. Team dimensions are standardized across the 80 window-1 team profiles. No style-fit score is computed here; it is computed in the ablation, where it is used, against a team profile that leaves the player out.
Alternatives considered: standardizing across all players; cosine similarity between raw player and club profiles, as in the original project.
Reason: Raw player profiles are dominated by position, so a similarity measure on them mostly measures position; within-group standard scores describe how a player differs from others in his role. A player is part of his own club's profile, so fit against that club needs the profile without him.

## 2026-09-25: Correction to the style profile dimensions entry
Decision: The entry "Style profile dimensions" of 2026-09-21 says set pieces are left out of every dimension. ## 2026-09-25: Player graph
Decision: One graph over the 2,073 window-1 player-team rows of the style profile table, built from window-1 games only, with two kinds of link between teammates. Passes: an open-play pass or cross whose result is success, received by the player of the next action in the same game and period when that action is by the same team and a different player; a pass with no such next action links no one. Pressing together: two pressure events by different players of the same team in the same game and period, at most 5 seconds apart. For each kind a pair's count covers both directions; each player keeps up to five partners with the highest counts, ties broken in favour of the lower player id, and only partners with a count of at least 3; a pair is linked if either player keeps the other. The two kinds are then merged into one set of links without weights or direction: 9,691 links, 3,680 by passes only, 1,768 by pressing only and 4,243 by both.
Alternatives considered: the original project's graph, whose nodes were players with 450 or more minutes and which had a third kind of link, possession exchanges between opponents; links weighted by their counts; a link for every pair with at least one pass.
Reason: Players under 450 minutes stay in the graph because removing them also removes links of the regulars they played with; the 450-minute filter applies where estimates are made, as elsewhere in this repo. A possession exchange links a player to opponents from other teams, which mostly records his position rather than his place in his own team. The graph layer used here averages a player's neighbours equally and takes no link weights, so link strength decides which links exist instead of weighting them; five partners with a floor of three keeps each player's main connections without linking him to everyone he passed to once. Pressing links need StatsBomb pressure events, so a Wyscout version of the graph can have pass links only.

## 2026-09-25: GraphSAGE embeddings
Decision: Each player-team row gets 16 numbers from a two-layer GraphSAGE network with mean aggregation, 32 hidden units and a ReLU between the layers. Its input is the row's eight within-group style standard scores, with missing scores set to 0, the group mean, and a one-hot of its position group; it has no action values. It is trained to tell linked pairs from unlinked pairs of the same team: a pair's score is the dot product of the two embeddings, the loss is binary cross-entropy, and each step draws as many unlinked same-team pairs as there are links. Training is full-batch Adam, learning rate 0.01, 200 steps, all fixed in advance and not tuned, on one CPU thread with deterministic algorithms and seed 0. The pooled embeddings come from one network trained on all four leagues. Each leave-one-league-out embedding comes from a network trained on the other three leagues and applied, without retraining, to the held-out league's rows and links.
Alternatives considered: training the network to predict action values; drawing unlinked pairs from the whole league; 64 dimensions, as in the original project; averaging embeddings over several seeds.
Reason: A training task without action values cannot leak the target the ablation tests, and keeping action values out of the input lets the ablation measure what a player's place in his team's network adds to what it already knows about his quality. Unlinked pairs drawn from the whole league would mostly teach the network which team a player is on; drawn from his own team, they make it learn who combines with whom. The ablation fits about 1,300 rows with noisy targets, where 64 more columns in a linear model invite overfitting. GraphSAGE embeds rows it was not trained on from their own features and links, which is what leave-one-league-out needs; as with the other leave-one-league-out results, the held-out league's first 23 matchdays are used. Embeddings from different seeds are not comparable coordinate by coordinate, so they cannot be averaged; fixed seeds and one thread make reruns exact, as for every other pinned result here.

## 2026-09-25: Embedding checks
Decision: Three checks, fixed before any embedding was computed. First, a rerun in a new process must reproduce the pooled embeddings exactly. Second, with a tenth of the links hidden from training and an equal number of unlinked same-team pairs set aside, the check passes if the pooled network ranks the hidden links above those pairs, measured by the area under the ROC curve, better than the same network trained on the same pairs with no links to pass messages along. Third, four more seeds of the pooled network are reported by the share of each player's ten nearest neighbours, among rows with at least 450 window-1 minutes, that seed 0 also finds; this one has no pass mark. Results: the rerun matched exactly; with 969 hidden links the area was 0.6763 with links and 0.7581 without, so the second check did not pass; the neighbour shares for seeds 1 to 4 were 0.1966, 0.1809, 0.2191 and 0.2084.
Alternatives considered: judging the embeddings by the ablation alone; a fixed threshold for the area.
Reason: The ablation tests whether the embeddings help predict. These checks test only that the embeddings are reproducible and that the links carry information beyond each player's own features, so a failure is found before the ablation is built on them. Comparing with the same network without links measures what the links add, which a fixed threshold would not.

## 2026-09-25: Scored links kept out of the message graph in training
Decision: From the second run on, each training step splits the training links at random: 30% of them, rounded down, are scored as positive pairs and the rest carry messages, re-drawn every step with the run's generator, and as many unlinked same-team pairs are drawn as there are scored links. The share is fixed in advance and not tuned. After training, embeddings pass messages along every training link. Nothing else in the network or its training changes; with a scored share of 0, every link is both scored and passed along, as in the first run, and the first run is reproduced exactly. The first run's network, scoring the hidden pairs of the second check with the hidden links added to the links it passes messages along, gave an area of 0.7913, against 0.6763 without them.
Alternatives considered: keeping the first run's embeddings and carrying the failed check into the ablation; one split made once rather than re-drawn each step; excluding a scored link from the message graph only when one of its ends has few links, as in the SpotTarget method of Zhu et al.
Reason: When the links a network is trained to predict are also the links it passes messages along, it can learn to recognise links present in its input, which does not carry over to links it cannot see; Zhu et al. (WSDM 2024) describe the overfitting and the mismatch between training and test that follow in graph link prediction. The first run showed that pattern: with links, the training loss at step 200 was lower than without (0.5302 against 0.5877), but the area on hidden links was lower too (0.6763 against 0.7581). Re-drawing the split each step lets every link be both scored and passed along over the course of training. One fix was chosen before the second run, so that the checks are not repeated until one passes.

## 2026-09-26: Ablation tiers
Decision: The ablation compares five nested ElasticNet models of the target of the entry "Evaluation design", each adding one family of window-1 inputs to the one before. T1, basic numbers: five position-group indicators, the log of window-1 minutes, and non-penalty goals, non-penalty shots and open-play assists per 90. T2 adds P3, the posterior mean of the entry "Baselines". T3 adds style: the player's eight standard scores and the eight standard scores of his team's profile without him. T4 adds style fit, the eight products of the entry "Style fit as an input". T5 adds the 16 GraphSAGE embedding columns. A goal is a shot or free-kick shot whose result is success; penalties count as neither goals nor shots, and own goals are not credited. An assist is a completed pass or cross after which every later action of the same sequence is by one teammate, the last being his non-penalty goal. No input is computed from window-2 data.
Alternatives considered: expected goals and expected assists in T1, read once from StatsBomb open data; no goals-based tier; team style entering in the same tier as fit; one model with every input and no ladder.
Reason: The ladder runs from what a basic statistics page shows to the inputs this project builds, so each step isolates one family. Style fit is an interaction between player and team, and an interaction can be measured only once both main effects are in the model, so the team's style enters in T3, a tier before fit; otherwise fit would take credit for team effects such as higher output on pressing teams. T4 against T3 is therefore the test of the club-fit claim, which the original project assumed rather than tested. T1 is the goals-based tier that the entry "Corrections to five entries of 2026-09-20" deferred to this ablation. Expected goals are left out: StatsBomb's expected-goals values are not in the cached tables and would need a second network read of all 1,517 league games, and a rating of another quantity, scored against a VAEP target, starts at a disadvantage that a linear fit only partly removes, so T1 against T2 cannot be read as a contest between expected goals and VAEP whatever T1 holds. Assists follow the shot-creation rule of the style profile, restricted to completed passes and crosses and to sequences that end in a non-penalty goal, so set-piece deliveries are not credited.

## 2026-09-26: Ablation evaluation
Decision: Every tier is scored on the 1,067 outfield pairs of the entry "Evaluation design" against its target, with pooled values on the pooled branch and leave-one-league-out values on that branch; the 76 goalkeeper pairs and the one pair of unknown group are left out. Predictions are out of fold. On the pooled branch the 80 teams are split into five groups, each holding four teams of every league, with each league's teams dealt in a random order from seed 0, and each group is predicted by a model fitted on the other four. On the leave-one-league-out branch each league is predicted by a model fitted on the other three. Every numeric input except P3 is centred within its league and position group, by the mean over that cell's outfield estimation-population rows (1,299 rows); P3 keeps the centring of the entry "Baselines", and position group enters as five indicators. The target, psi2, P0, P2 and P3 are read from the stored evaluation tables.
Alternatives considered: a split of players rather than teams; leave-one-team-out; including goalkeepers; inputs centred by league only; inputs left uncentred.
Reason: With 4c's target and rows, the tiers are measured on the same footing as its baselines. This is the first fitted step that reads window-2 values, because a model has to learn from targets; no prediction comes from a model that read its own team's window-2 rows, or on the leave-one-league-out branch its own league's. With league and window levels removed from the target, what a model can carry over from other teams' window-2 results is the relation between window-1 inputs and later output. Teammates share opponents, a schedule and a way of playing, so a split of players would let a model learn a team's window-2 level from teammates of the players it predicts; the bootstrap unit is the team for the same reason. Five groups keep refitting inside every bootstrap redraw affordable, where leave-one-team-out would need 80 fits per tier. Goalkeepers are too few for a model of their own and their corrected TSE in 4c was near or below zero, and the pair of unknown group has no style scores. Against a target with league levels removed, an input's league-level differences can only add error: across the 80 team profiles, between-league differences account for 24.9% of the variance of pressing, 29.2% of dribbling, 22.0% of creation and 19.4% of depth, and at most 7.5% for each of the other four dimensions, and these data cannot separate league style from differences in how each league's events were recorded. Leaving a player out of his team's profile shifts it by position, as removing a winger narrows the rest of the team, so the centre is taken within position group too, and the indicators carry each group's level. Only window-1 rows set a centre. On the leave-one-league-out branch the held-out league is centred on its own window-1 rows, which the entry "Leakage rule for leave-one-league-out, amended" allows, and this also removes any shift in the embeddings of a league its network never trained on.

## 2026-09-26: Ablation tuning
Decision: Each tier is fitted with scikit-learn's ElasticNetCV, version 1.9.1, with an intercept, on inputs rescaled to mean 0 and standard deviation 1 over the training rows. The penalty's mix is chosen from 0.1, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99 and 1, and its strength from the library's default path of 100 values, by mean squared error over five inner folds of whole teams formed from the training rows only; every other setting is the library default. Rows held out of a fit enter neither the rescaling nor the tuning.
Alternatives considered: least squares without a penalty; ridge or lasso alone; inner folds of players; a finer grid.
Reason: Up to 50 inputs, several of them nearly collinear, are fitted to noisy targets on the training part of 1,067 rows, which calls for a penalty, and ElasticNet lets the data choose between shrinking correlated inputs together and dropping weak ones. The mix values are the list the scikit-learn documentation suggests. Inner folds of whole teams keep teammates on one side of each tuning split, as in the outer split. ElasticNetCV does not rescale inputs itself. Squared error is the right tuning score because a row's variance correction is the same for every model, so the lower squared error is the lower TSE.

## 2026-09-26: Style fit as an input
Decision: Style fit enters the ablation as eight products, one per dimension, of the player's standard score and the standard score of his team's profile without him, each centred within league and position group before multiplying; the products are centred the same way. The profile without him follows the definitions of the entry "Team profiles from team actions" on the team's window-1 data less the units his own profile is built from: his actions for possession, width, verticality, depth, dribbling and shot creation; the sequences he took part in, through any of his actions, for build-up speed; and his pressure events for pressing, with the rest of the team's pressing scaled by the team's window-1 player minutes over those minutes less his. Sequences, their durations and build-up times, and the shot-creation flags are formed from the team's full action stream before anything is removed. The profile without him is standardized with the means and standard deviations of the 80 stored team profiles. Missing player scores are set to 0, the group mean, as for the embedding inputs: 0 values over the 1,299 outfield estimation rows. With nobody removed, the same code reproduces every stored team profile value to within 0.0.
Alternatives considered: a single fit score, the cosine similarity of the two eight-number profiles, which the original project computed on raw profiles; removing only the player's actions for every dimension, build-up speed included; the team's full profile.
Reason: A player's standard scores measure how he differs from others in his position group, and his team's how it differs from other teams. Both are in standard deviations, so a product is positive when player and team lean the same way, negative when they lean apart and near zero when either is ordinary. Separate products let the model weigh the dimensions: across the 80 team profiles, possession, build-up speed and verticality are close to one axis, with absolute correlations of 0.92, 0.94 and 0.86, which one similarity score would count two to three times. Each dimension leaves out the units the player's own score is built from, so no action, sequence or pressure event counts on both sides of a product; build-up speed is a property of whole sequences, so removing only his actions would keep sequences he shaped in the team's value. Pressing is a count, and without the minutes scaling the rest of the team would look less active only because one player's events were removed. Fit is measured with the player's current club over the later part of the same season: the open data hold no consecutive league seasons, so fit after a transfer cannot be measured, and a positive result supports the recruitment claim only indirectly.

## 2026-09-26: Ablation success rule
Decision: Fixed before any tier was fitted: a tier passes if, on the pooled branch, its squared error summed over the outfield pairs is below that of the model it is compared with in at least 1,950 of 2,000 bootstrap redraws; a redraw in which the two are equal counts against it. T1 is compared with P0, each later tier with the tier below it, and T5 also with T2. The redraws are the team bootstrap of the entry "Bootstrap unit": each league's 20 teams drawn with replacement from seed 0, the same draws for every model and both branches. Each redraw refits the tier models from the drawn rows, the five-group split, rescaling and tuning included. The action-value models, calibrators, shrinkage fits, league levels, style profiles, input centres and embeddings are held fixed, so the results are conditional on them. The leave-one-league-out branch is judged by the same rule and reported beside the pooled branch, and a tier that passes on the pooled branch but not on the other is described as not carrying over to a new league. Per-league results are reported and flagged as resting on that league's twenty teams. Each tier is also reported by its TSE ratio to P2, corrected and uncorrected, and to P3, with 95% percentile intervals, by Spearman correlation and calibration slope as in 4c, and by the inputs its model keeps when fitted on all rows, as description only. The ladder is described as monotonic only if every step passes.
Alternatives considered: the ratio rule of the entry "Evaluation design"; scoring one fixed set of out-of-fold predictions in every redraw; the leave-one-league-out branch as the headline.
Reason: Passing in at least 1,950 of 2,000 redraws is the upper end of the 95% percentile interval for the difference in squared error lying below zero. Within a redraw, TSE and squared error differ by the same sum of psi2 for every model, so this is 4c's rule that the upper end of the interval for a TSE ratio lies below 1, stated on the difference, which stays readable when a corrected TSE comes near zero, as it did for goalkeepers in 4c. T5 against T2 lets gains too small for any single step to show count together. Scoring fixed predictions would leave out the variation that refitting adds, and intervals for cross-validated error that leave it out come out too narrow (Bates, Hastie and Tibshirani, Journal of the American Statistical Association, 2024). P3 is held fixed here rather than refit, so its interval in this ablation differs from the one in the entry "Bootstrap unit". The pooled branch is the headline, as in 4c.

## 2026-09-26: Embeddings in the ablation
Decision: T5 adds the 16 embedding columns as they are, centred and rescaled like every other input. The pooled branch uses the pooled embeddings, and each leave-one-league-out fold uses its own network's embeddings both for the rows it fits and for the rows it predicts. T5's verdict uses seed 0, the stored embeddings. Networks trained from seeds 1 to 4 with the frozen settings of the entry "Scored links kept out of the message graph in training" are reported on all rows, by the difference in squared error between T5 and T4 on each branch, without redraws; if T5 passes with seed 0 and any of seeds 1 to 4 gives a difference at or above zero, the pass is reported as seed-dependent. The networks are not retrained inside the redraws. Every other random choice in the ablation uses seed 0, and the ElasticNet fits involve no randomness.
Alternatives considered: embeddings averaged over seeds; predictions averaged over seeds; redraws for every seed; fewer columns through principal components.
Reason: Embeddings from different seeds are not comparable coordinate by coordinate, so they cannot be averaged, and averaging predictions over seeds would test a model other than the pinned one. The other seeds, scored on all rows, show how far the seed alone moves T5's gain, at a fraction of the cost of redrawing for each. Retraining the networks inside every redraw would change what is measured, as refitting the rating models would have in 4c. The penalty handles 16 columns without reducing them first.

## 2026-09-26: Build-up speed left out of the team side of the ablation
Decision: The team profile without the player enters the ablation without build-up speed, so T3 adds the player's eight standard scores and seven of his team's, T4 adds seven fit products and T5 has 48 inputs; the player's own build-up score stays in T3. This amends the entries "Ablation tiers" and "Style fit as an input", and was decided before any tier was fitted. Alternatives considered: keeping build-up speed on the team side as committed; the team's full build-up speed, with nobody removed.
Reason: A long sequence is more likely than a short one to include any given player, so the sequences a player took no part in are shorter and faster than his team's sequences in general, whatever his style. Leaving out his sequences made the team's build-up faster for 1,280 of the 1,299 outfield estimation rows, by a median of 0.46 and at most 3.29 team standard deviations, and the change rises with the share of the team's build-up sequences he took part in (Spearman correlation 0.77). The team's build-up without him therefore measures his involvement as well as his team, and a fit product built on it could credit fit with an effect of his involvement. The team's full build-up speed would count his own sequences on both sides of the product, which leaving him out was meant to prevent. Across the 80 team profiles build-up speed lies close to one axis with possession and verticality (absolute correlations 0.92 and 0.86), and their profiles without the player leave out only his own actions, so the team's place on that axis stays in the model.
